The Prophet and the Plant

When Jonah saw that God had forgiven Nineveh, he became angry.

Very angry.

Instead of rejoicing that the people had repented, Jonah was upset that God had shown them mercy.

“That is why I tried to run away before,” Jonah complained to the Lord.
“I knew You are gracious and compassionate, slow to anger and full of mercy.”

Jonah had wanted Nineveh to be punished.

But God had chosen forgiveness instead.

Feeling bitter, Jonah left the city and sat on a hill overlooking Nineveh. There he built a small shelter and waited, hoping to see what would happen to the city.

The hot desert sun beat down on him as he sat alone outside the walls.

Then God caused a leafy plant to grow quickly beside Jonah.

Its broad leaves spread shade over him, protecting him from the burning heat.

For the first time in many days, Jonah felt comfortable and happy.

But the next morning, God sent a worm to attack the plant, and it withered away.

Soon a scorching east wind blew across the desert.

The sun beat heavily upon Jonah’s head until he became weak and miserable.

Again Jonah complained,

“It would be better for me to die than to live.”

Then God spoke gently to him.

“Is it right for you to be angry about the plant?”

“Yes,” Jonah answered.
“I am angry enough to die.”

But the Lord replied,

“You cared so much about this plant, though you did nothing to make it grow. It appeared in one day and died in one day.”

Then God revealed the lesson Jonah needed to learn.

“And should I not care about Nineveh — that great city filled with thousands of people who do not know right from wrong?”

Jonah had cared more about his own comfort than about the lives of people.

But God’s mercy was greater than Jonah’s anger.

The story ends there — quietly, with a question.

A reminder that God’s compassion reaches farther than human pride, and His grace can even extend to enemies willing to repent.
