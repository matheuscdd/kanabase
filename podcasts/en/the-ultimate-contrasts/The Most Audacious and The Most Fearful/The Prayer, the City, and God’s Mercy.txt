The Prayer, the City, and God’s Mercy

Inside the great fish, Jonah sat alone in darkness.

Cold water surrounded him.
Seaweed tangled around him.
Far above, the waves crashed over the ocean.

For three days and three nights, Jonah remained there.

At last, Jonah cried out to God in prayer.

“In my distress I called to the Lord,
and He answered me,” he prayed.

Jonah admitted that he had run away from God and disobeyed His command.

Yet even from the depths of the sea, he realized God had not abandoned him.

“With thankful praise,
I will fulfill my promises to You,” Jonah said.
“Salvation comes from the Lord.”

Then God commanded the great fish, and it spit Jonah safely onto dry land.

Once again, the word of the Lord came to Jonah:

“Go to the great city of Nineveh and proclaim the message I give you.”

This time, Jonah obeyed.

So he traveled to Nineveh, a city so large it took several days to cross. Crowds filled the streets, merchants sold goods in busy markets, and soldiers guarded the city gates.

Walking through the city, Jonah lifted his voice and warned the people:

“In forty days, Nineveh will be destroyed!”

The message spread quickly through the city.

To Jonah’s surprise, the people believed him.

From the greatest to the least, they humbled themselves before God. They put on rough clothing called sackcloth and began to fast as a sign of repentance.

Even the king of Nineveh rose from his throne.

He removed his royal robes, sat in ashes, and issued a command throughout the city:

“Let everyone turn away from evil and violence. Perhaps God will show mercy and not destroy us.”

The entire city cried out to God.

And when God saw that the people truly repented and turned from their wickedness, He showed them mercy.

The destruction that had been announced did not come.

The God Jonah tried to run from had not only rescued the prophet from the sea…

He had also rescued an entire city willing to repent.
