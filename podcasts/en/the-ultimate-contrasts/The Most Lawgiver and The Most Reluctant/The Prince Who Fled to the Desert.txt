The Prince Who Fled to the Desert

Moses grew up in the palace of Egypt, surrounded by wealth, power, and the wisdom of Pharaoh’s court.

But deep inside, he never forgot who he truly was.

He was a Hebrew.

One day, after Moses had become a man, he went out to see the suffering of his people. There he watched an Egyptian slave master beating a Hebrew worker without mercy.

Moses looked around carefully.

No one seemed to be watching.

Filled with anger, he struck the Egyptian and killed him. Then he hid the body in the sand.

The next day, Moses returned and saw two Hebrews fighting with each other.

“Why are you hurting your fellow Hebrew?” Moses asked.

But the man answered angrily,

“Who made you ruler and judge over us? Are you planning to kill me like you killed the Egyptian?”

Fear rushed through Moses.

He realized the secret was no longer hidden.

Soon Pharaoh himself heard what had happened. Furious, he ordered Moses to be killed.

So Moses fled from Egypt.

Leaving behind the palace, the riches, and the life he had always known, he escaped into the wilderness of Midian.

The desert stretched endlessly before him — dry winds, burning sand, and lonely mountains beneath the open sky.

At last, Moses stopped beside a well.

There he saw seven young women arriving to draw water for their father’s flock. But some shepherds pushed them away and tried to drive them off.

Moses stood up and defended the women.

Then he helped water their sheep.

When the women returned home early, their father, Reuel, was surprised.

“How did you return so quickly today?” he asked.

They told him about the Egyptian who had rescued them.

Reuel invited Moses into his home, and Moses stayed with the family.

In time, Moses married Reuel’s daughter Zipporah.

Far from Egypt, Moses began a new life as a shepherd in the quiet wilderness.

But although Moses believed he had left his past behind…

God was preparing him for something far greater than he could imagine.
