Boaz Redeems Ruth

Early the next morning, Boaz went to the town gate.

The town gate was where important decisions were made and where people gathered to discuss business.

Boaz sat down and waited.

Soon, the closer relative Boaz had spoken about passed by.

“My friend, come sit here,” Boaz called.

Then Boaz gathered ten of the town elders to witness the conversation.

When everyone was seated, Boaz explained the situation.

“Naomi is selling the land that belonged to our relative Elimelech,” Boaz said.
“As the closest family redeemer, you have the first right to buy it.”

At first, the man answered,

“I will redeem it.”

But then Boaz added,

“If you redeem the land, you must also marry Ruth the Moabite, so the family line of the dead may continue.”

The man became hesitant.

“I cannot redeem it,” he said,
“because it may affect my own inheritance.”

So he removed his sandal and gave it to Boaz, which was the custom in Israel to confirm an agreement.

Then Boaz stood before the elders and all the people.

“You are witnesses today,” he announced.
“I have bought all that belonged to Elimelech and his family.
And I will take Ruth as my wife.”

The people blessed Boaz and Ruth with joyful words.

“May the Lord bless this woman entering your home,” they said.

So Boaz married Ruth.

And God blessed them with a son.

Naomi held the baby in her arms with great joy.

The women of Bethlehem celebrated with her.

“Praise the Lord!” they said.
“Naomi now has a redeemer for her family.”

The baby was named Obed.

And one day, Obed would become the grandfather of a great king of Israel:

King David.

What began with sorrow, loss, and loneliness…

Ended with hope, family, and blessing.

And through Ruth’s faithfulness, God was preparing a story far greater than anyone could yet see.
