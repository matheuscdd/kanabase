A Story of Loss and Loyalty

Long ago, during the time of the judges, a severe famine struck Israel. Food became scarce, and families struggled to survive.

In Bethlehem lived Elimelech, his wife Naomi, and their two sons, Mahlon and Kilion. Hoping for a better future, they left their home and traveled to the land of Moab.

At first, they likely believed the move would be temporary. But as the years passed, tragedy came. Elimelech died, leaving Naomi alone with her sons.

Later, Mahlon and Kilion married Moabite women named Orpah and Ruth. Yet sorrow struck again when both sons also died.

Now Naomi, Orpah, and Ruth were left as widows together in a foreign land.

Then Naomi heard that the famine in Bethlehem had ended, and she decided to return home. Orpah and Ruth began the journey with her, but along the road Naomi stopped and urged them to stay in Moab.

“You are still young,” she told them sadly. “Return to your families and rebuild your lives.”

The women wept together. Orpah eventually kissed Naomi goodbye and returned home, but Ruth refused to leave.

She spoke words that would never be forgotten:

“Where you go, I will go.
Where you stay, I will stay.
Your people will be my people,
and your God my God.”

Ruth chose loyalty over comfort and stayed beside Naomi.

So the two women traveled together to Bethlehem. When they arrived, the town was stirred to see Naomi again.

But Naomi said sorrowfully,

“Do not call me Naomi, which means pleasant.
Call me Mara, because my life has become bitter.”

Though Naomi felt empty and broken, she did not return alone. Ruth stood faithfully beside her.

And even in their grief, God was already preparing a new beginning for both of them.
