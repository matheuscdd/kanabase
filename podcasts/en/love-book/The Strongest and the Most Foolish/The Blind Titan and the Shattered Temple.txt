The Blind Titan and the Shattered Temple

[description] In the crushing, suffocating darkness of the Gaza dungeon, the fallen champion became a beast of burden. Blinded and bound in heavy bronze fetters, Samson pushed the massive stone grinding wheel day after endless day. Yet, unnoticed by his cruel captors amidst the sweat and the dust, the thick hair on his scarred head silently began to grow again.

[humiliation] High above his damp cell, a deafening roar of triumph echoed through the city. Thousands of Philistine lords, commanders, and civilians had gathered in the colossal temple of their god, Dagon, celebrating a grand sacrifice. Drunk on wine and arrogance, they demanded the legendary, broken Israelite be dragged from the darkness to entertain them.

[tension] The blind giant was paraded before the jeering, spitting crowd. Weary and battered, the mockery echoing in his sightless world, he asked the young servant boy leading him by the hand to let him feel the massive central pillars that supported the entire, crowded structure, including the three thousand spectators roaring from the roof above.

[resolution] Leaning his scarred back against the cold, heavy stone, Samson bowed his head. From the absolute depths of his broken spirit, he whispered a final, desperate prayer to the heavens: "O Lord God, remember me, I pray. Strengthen me, just this once, that I may take one vengeance for my two eyes."

[climax] He planted his right hand on one pillar, and his left hand on the other. Bracing his enormous frame, he cried out over the deafening noise of the crowd, "Let me die with the Philistines!" With a terrifying, monstrous surge of returning divine power, he pushed forward with all his might. The stone cracked. The massive pillars snapped like dry twigs. With a catastrophic, thunderous roar, the towering roof caved in, burying the lords, the screaming crowd, and the champion himself under thousands of tons of shattering stone.

[epilogue] In his dying breath, the blind judge delivered his most devastating blow, slaying more of his enemies in his final, apocalyptic sacrifice than he ever had in his entire life of war. Later, in the solemn quiet of the dusty ruins, his brothers and his father's household descended to carry his broken body home, burying him in the tomb of his father, Manoah, leaving his legendary story etched into the earth forever.
