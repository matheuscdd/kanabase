The Child Hidden in the River

Long ago, the people of Israel lived in Egypt.

At first, they were welcomed there. But as the years passed, the Israelites became very numerous, and a new Pharaoh began to fear them.

“What if they become stronger than us?” he asked.

So Pharaoh made the Israelites slaves. They were forced to work hard building cities and carrying heavy loads beneath the burning sun.

But even through suffering, the Israelites continued to grow in number.

Pharaoh became even more afraid.

In his cruelty, he gave a terrible command: every Hebrew baby boy that was born must be thrown into the Nile River.

During this dark time, a Hebrew woman gave birth to a son.

When she saw the child, she knew he was special.

For three months, she hid him carefully inside her home, keeping him quiet whenever danger came near.

But as the baby grew, hiding him became impossible.

So his mother made a small basket from reeds and covered it with tar so it could float on water.

With tears in her eyes, she placed her baby inside the basket and set it among the tall reeds beside the Nile River.

Nearby, the baby’s sister watched from a distance, hoping to see what would happen.

Not long after, Pharaoh’s daughter came down to the river to bathe.

As she walked along the water’s edge, she noticed the basket hidden among the reeds.

When she opened it, she found the crying baby.

Immediately, her heart filled with compassion.

“This is one of the Hebrew children,” she said softly.

At that moment, the baby’s sister bravely stepped forward.

“Should I find a Hebrew woman to care for the child?” she asked.

Pharaoh’s daughter agreed.

And so, the baby’s own mother was brought back to care for him safely.

Years later, Pharaoh’s daughter adopted the boy as her own son.

She named him Moses, saying,

“I drew him out of the water.”

The child who had once floated helplessly in a river would one day lead an entire nation to freedom.
