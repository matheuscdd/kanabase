Wisdom and the Two Mothers

After King David died, his son Solomon became king over Israel.

Though he ruled a great kingdom, Solomon felt young and inexperienced.

One night, God appeared to him in a dream and said,

“Ask for whatever you want Me to give you.”

Solomon did not ask for riches, power, or victory over enemies.

Instead, he answered humbly,

“Give me wisdom so I may lead Your people well.”

God was pleased with Solomon’s request. He promised to give him great wisdom, along with riches and honor.

Soon, everyone in Israel began to see that God’s wisdom was upon the king.

One day, two women came before Solomon arguing over a baby boy.

Both claimed to be the child’s mother.

During the night, one woman’s baby had died, and now each insisted the living child belonged to her.

“There are no witnesses,” the people said.
“No one knows the truth.”

The courtroom filled with confusion.

Then Solomon calmly ordered,

“Bring me a sword.”

The room fell silent.

“Cut the living child in two,” Solomon said,
“and give half to each woman.”

Immediately, one woman cried out,

“No! Please give her the child — just do not kill him!”

But the other woman replied coldly,

“Divide him.”

At once, Solomon knew the truth.

“Give the baby to the first woman,” he declared.
“She is the true mother.”

The real mother had revealed herself through love and compassion.

When the people heard of Solomon’s judgment, they stood in awe, realizing that God had truly given their king extraordinary wisdom.
