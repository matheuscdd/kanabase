The Prophet Who Ran Away

Long ago, God spoke to a prophet named Jonah.

“Go to the great city of Nineveh,” God commanded.
“Warn the people there, because their wickedness has become very great.”

But Jonah did not want to go.

Nineveh was a powerful and cruel city, and Jonah feared its people. Instead of obeying God, he decided to run away.

So Jonah traveled to the port city of Joppa, where he found a ship preparing to sail toward Tarshish — a place far from Nineveh.

He paid the fare, boarded the ship, and sailed away, hoping to escape from the Lord’s command.

As the ship crossed the sea, dark clouds suddenly gathered overhead.

A violent storm crashed against the water.

The wind howled.
Waves slammed into the ship.
The sailors became terrified.

Each man cried out to his own god while desperately throwing cargo into the sea to lighten the ship.

But Jonah had gone below deck and fallen asleep.

The captain rushed to him in disbelief.

“How can you sleep at a time like this?” he shouted.
“Pray to your God! Maybe He will save us!”

The sailors realized the storm was unnatural, so they cast lots to discover who was responsible.

The lot fell on Jonah.

Fear spread across the ship.

“Who are you?” the sailors demanded.
“What have you done?”

Jonah answered honestly,

“I am a Hebrew, and I worship the Lord, the God who made the sea and the land. I am running away from Him.”

The men became even more afraid.

“What should we do to stop the storm?” they asked.

Jonah looked at the raging sea and said quietly,

“Throw me into the water, and the storm will stop.”

At first, the sailors refused. They fought hard to row back to land, but the storm only grew stronger.

Finally, with heavy hearts, they picked Jonah up and threw him into the sea.

The moment Jonah hit the water, the storm became calm.

The sailors stood in awe of God’s power.

Meanwhile, Jonah sank into the dark depths of the sea beneath the crashing waves.

But God had not abandoned him.

The Lord prepared a great fish, which swallowed Jonah whole.

And there, alive inside the darkness of the fish, Jonah remained for three days and three nights.
