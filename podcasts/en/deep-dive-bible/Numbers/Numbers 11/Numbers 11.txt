We analyzed the complete psychological
breakdown of a strong leader trying to manage an irrational crowd.
We saw the beautiful, elegant solution of sharing the spiritual burden with 70
elders, and we witnessed the devastating reality of a group getting exactly what
they demanded, only to find that their crating was a trap.
The ancient writers, as Alter shows us, were masters of using dialogue,
silence, and sudden shifts in focus to make us think, and the theological
commentaries remind us that the choices we make about our daily habits and
appetites have a profound impact on our spiritual and mental health.
As we conclude this deep dive, it is vital to apply these concepts.
This text acts as a mirror for human behavior.
It asks you to examine the group dynamics you participate in.
It asks if you are falling into the nostalgia trap.
Looking backward at a toxic situation and convincing yourself
it was better than the quiet, healthy life you have now.
Think about the manna in your own life.
What are the simple, consistent, healthy things you currently possess
that you are ignoring because you are bored?
Are you demanding a sharper, more intense experience, even if you know it comes with a high cost?
The obsession is the poison.
So consider the things you are fiercely craving right now, the things you are convinced you must have to be satisfied.
If those things suddenly fell from the sky and piled up three feet deep all around you, would they bring you peace or would they become a burden that crushes you?
Sometimes the simple things we already have are exactly what we need to survive.
